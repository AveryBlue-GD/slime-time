# Dry Trying: marketing brief

Written 2026-10-20 for the social-media push (a claude.ai Project for marketing work starts from this file).
Keep it short and true; every claim below is checked against the game.

## The game in one line
**Dry Trying**: a slime glides through pitch-black, fog-filled mazes. Its goo trail lights the way, but every
move drains it. Find the golden chalice before you dry up.

- Tagline: **ESCAPE THE FOG / BEFORE YOU DRY UP**
- Formerly "Slime Time" (renamed 2026-10-19; there are other games called Slime Time on Steam).
- Look: 320x180 pixel art, gooey lettering (the logo drips differently every time the title opens).
- Platforms: Windows, macOS, Linux, web browser (itch.io). Phones later. A free demo is built (see below).
- Languages: English now; 17 more are built and switched off until the game is final.

## What to pitch
1. **The core loop**: glide, leap, splat off walls, light up the maze with your trail, budget your slime.
2. **10 themed story levels**: Dark Dungeon, Mossy Mines, Sandstone Sanctum, Sunken Shrine, Toxic Tunnels,
   Creepy Catacombs, Frozen Fortress, Obsidian Oubliette, Crystal Caverns, Volcanic Vault. 165 hidden coins.
3. **A replayable endgame**: endless random mazes (they pay tickets), hard mode with its own mazes, best
   times and gold / silver / bronze medals on every level.
4. **A grind for cosmetics**: 13 skins, each with a see-through jelly version earned on hard mode; 12 hats won on
   a prize wheel; goo trails; map papers. Over 1,000 combinations to dress your slime.
5. A wordless intro and a short tutorial (SPLASHDOWN) that teaches by playing.

## Rules for every post, trailer and store page
- **Never mention or show HEAT** (the hard-mode modifiers): the owner doesn't plan on using it. Don't mention
  DESCENT either (switched off).
- **The RAINBOW and STOPWATCH skins are secrets**: only ever shown as black locked silhouettes, never in colour.
  (The rainbow HAT is fine.)
- Each level is shown with its own skin: Lime L1, Mint L2, Lemon L3, Ocean L4, Grape L5, Crimson L6, Frost L7,
  Inkky L8, Bubblegum L9, Ember L10.
- Use real game footage. Keep on-screen text short and simple (it will be translated).
- Don't promise that demo progress carries over to the full game.

## Assets ready now
- **Three vertical Shorts** (1080x1920, 22-28 s, real gameplay, full screen, captions in the logo's gooey
  lettering, no HUD), each with a music version:
  - A, "Can you escape before you dry up?": the dark, the trail, levels, then endgame and cosmetics.
  - B, cosmetics first: "1000+ ways to dress your slime", hats, trails, papers, then "earn them all".
  - C, "Beat the story... then the real game starts": the endgame loop and the secret skin.
- **Music mockups** (made in code from the game's own goo sounds, no licensing issues): three hub loops (Cozy Goo,
  Foggy Lullaby, Bouncy Hub) and two trailer tracks (Goo Rush, Fog Pulse).
- Contact sheets (one frame per beat) for each Short.
- How they're made and changed: docs/marketing/trailer.md and music.md in the repo.

## The demo (built, not released)
Free demo from the same code: the intro, the tutorial and **levels 1-4** (web: 1-3), their coins and skins,
capped upgrades whose prices use exactly the demo's coins, hard level 1, three paying random mazes after
level 4, the prize wheel (unlocked by finishing the demo, 2 hats max). Later levels are locked and **cut from
the demo files**. A "DEMO" sticker on the title. An end screen teases later areas and has a WISHLIST button
that appears once a store link exists.

## Status and next steps
- No Steam page yet. Research (docs/advisor/demo.md) says: get the Steam page live early, release the demo
  weeks before Steam Next Fest (early demos get about 2.5x the median festival wishlists), make the end screen
  the wishlist pitch, and use a shorter web demo for traffic from Shorts.
- Open: pick the best Short and music pairing, choose a hub music loop, write store page text, set the
  wishlist link, and plan the posting schedule per language.
